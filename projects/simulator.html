Water Rocket Simulator

Change the design, launch it on the screen, and see what the physics predicts.

This is a model, not your data. The simulator is for making predictions, comparing design choices, and checking your reasoning. Your lab report must use the measurements from your own rocket's flight. When your real results differ from the simulation, that difference is something to explain in your discussion.

Ideas for Using the Simulator

  • Before you build: Use it to decide on a water volume and to write a reasoned height prediction for your introduction. Explain your prediction with the models in your report, not just the number from the screen.
  • Change one thing at a time: The Design Explorer is a graph you can interpret. Why does the height go up and then down as you add water?
  • Turn air resistance off: Set the drag coefficient to 0. This is the constant-acceleration model from Unit 1. How far off is it? Look at the rise time and the fall time.
  • After launch day: Enter your rocket's measurements and compare the simulated hang time with your measured one. Then adjust the drag coefficient and the nozzle flow coefficient until they agree. What does that suggest about your real rocket?

What the Numbers Mean

  • Thrust phase: The time while water is leaving the nozzle. This is the phase you time on slow-motion video. After the water is gone, the compressed air left in the bottle escapes for a short time and gives a smaller extra push (“total thrust time”).
  • Average acceleration: The speed at the end of the thrust phase divided by the thrust time.
  • Thrust-to-weight ratio: The thrust force at the start divided by the weight of the loaded rocket.
  • Height from hang time: The value you get by assuming the rise and fall take equal times, y = ½g(t/2)². Compare it with the simulated height to see how much that assumption matters.
  • Terminal speed: The speed at which air resistance would equal the weight of the empty rocket. A falling rocket gets close to it.

How the Model Works & Its Limits

Show the physics
  • Water phase: The air above the water expands without exchanging heat (PV1.4 = constant). The water leaves at the speed set by the pressure difference, so the thrust is about 2 × (nozzle area) × (pressure above the outside air), scaled by the flow coefficient.
  • Air phase: After the water is gone, the remaining compressed air escapes through the nozzle as a gas (choked flow at higher pressures).
  • Motion: The rocket's mass changes as water leaves. At every 0.2 ms time step the program finds the net force (thrust minus weight minus air drag), calculates the acceleration, and updates the velocity and position. Air drag is 1⁄2ρCdAv².
  • Launch guide: The rocket is held along the launch angle for the first 0.3 m of travel, then the thrust follows the direction of motion.

Limitations

  • The rocket is treated as a single object that always points where it is moving. Wobble, tumbling, and fin flutter are not modeled.
  • Losses in your launcher (valves, hoses, tight fittings), foaming of the water, and sloshing are not modeled directly. They are part of why real rockets often fall short of a simulation. The flow coefficient and drag coefficient are how you can include them.
  • Wind is ignored. Air density is fixed at 1.2 kg/m³ and the air starts at 20 °C.
  • Never use the simulator to decide a real launch pressure. Follow the pressure limit and safety rules set by your teacher.
